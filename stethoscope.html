Emotional Stethoscope

A doctor uses a stethoscope to listen to your heart. The Emotional Stethoscope helps children listen to their feelings, so they can understand them, share them and take care of them.

Five parts

What's inside the Stethoscope

It could live in a smartwatch, a phone or a tablet.

1

The "How am I feeling?" button

A 60-second check-in. Where do I feel tight or heavy? What just happened? What's in my mind? What do I wish could change?

2

The feelings wheel

Pick a colour: red for angry, blue for sad, green for calm, yellow for worried, purple for too much. Then get a kind tip.

3

The body map

Tap where you feel it: head, chest or tummy. This joins body and mind.

4

Voice notes and journal

Say or write "This happened and I felt ___". Keep it, or share it with a parent or teacher later.

5

Take Care Now

Slow breathing, a stretch, or touching five things around you. Plus a reminder: "You're safe. You're strong."

How it might sense feelings

Gentle sensors

  • Heart rate: notices stress or worry.
  • Skin warmth: notices changes linked to feelings.
  • Skin response: notices when feelings get strong.
  • Movement: notices fidgeting or restlessness.
Our promise

Private feelings stay private

A child's feelings belong to the child. Only growth and good moments are ever celebrated with others. The goal is self-awareness, sharing feelings safely, and knowing what to do next.

Where it comes from

Part of every 3rd Human session

In sessions, children build their own "stethoscope" and their own emergency kit for hard days. EmoNav is the first app to grow from this idea.
