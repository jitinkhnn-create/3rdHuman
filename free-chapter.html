Meeting Your Inner Voice

Section 1 of Meeting Myself: The Tools You Already Carry, by Jitin Khanna. Read it here, free. It takes about 20 minutes, and there are three small practices to try along the way.

Let's Begin Where Everything Starts: Your Self-Talk

Let's start from the place where many things in our life are first realized: our self-talk.

Have you ever thought about this?

Who is the person you talk to the most in your entire life?
Who is the one you listen to more than anyone else?
Whose opinion do you trust the most?

Surprisingly, the answer is you.

It is the quiet conversation constantly happening inside your mind. It speaks to you all day long.

Sometimes it encourages you.
Sometimes it questions you.
Sometimes it even stops you, along with a feeling of not actually stopping.

This constant inner conversation is called self-talk.

Your self-talk is like a voice that guides us and makes us believe that whatever it says is true. It becomes such a regular part of your thinking that most of the time you don't even notice it.

Yet it quietly influences many of our decisions.

Think of it like a magical genie living inside you: a powerful guide that has been placed within you.

But there is one important rule. To use this power fully, you must learn to guide your inner voice rather than always letting it guide you.

Sometimes this voice can be playful or even tricky. It may persuade you to delay things, make excuses, or avoid tasks that could help you grow.

It may whisper things like:
"Do it later."
"It's too difficult."
"You can't do it."

And slowly, these small thoughts begin shaping your actions.

But here is the powerful truth. That same inner voice can also become your greatest supporter. It can motivate you, guide you, and push you toward becoming the best version of yourself.

Your self-talk is one of the most powerful tools you have to shape your life.

Throughout this book, you will begin to notice how this automatic inner voice influences many parts of your life: what you choose to do, how you react to situations, whom you trust, when you speak, and when you stay silent.

Imagine watching a football match while a commentator describes everything happening on the field.

Now imagine having a commentator living inside your mind. Every situation receives commentary.

When you make a mistake, it has something to say.
When you succeed, it has something to say.
When somebody criticizes you, it has something to say.
When you feel afraid, excited, confused, or happy, it immediately starts talking.

The interesting part is that many of us never question what this commentator says. We believe it.

Most of the time, we accept the first thought that appears in our mind as the truth.

But the first thought is not always correct.

Often, it is simply an automatic reaction rather than a thoughtful response. It may not represent the full truth, the full information, or the best decision.

Learning to observe and guide your inner voice can completely change the way you experience life.

Remember this simple idea:

Your dreams are the destination.
You are the vehicle.
And your inner voice is the fuel that moves your vehicle forward.

If the fuel is positive, clear, and focused, your journey becomes smoother and stronger.

Now let us explore how this inner voice has been shaping who we are, and how we can learn to use it wisely.

Journey From "Who I Am" To "What I Really Am"

Most of the time, we notice and remember the things that did not go the way we wanted them to. We focus on what did not work instead of what did. Because of this, we constantly try to move from a situation that is not working to one that does.

Sometimes, feeling sad or discouraged becomes a place we end up in without even wanting to. In such moments, we begin to question almost everything in our lives.

This happens to everyone, and it can happen at any time.

At times, we feel the need to hide certain parts of ourselves just to "fit in" or belong. Sometimes we may not even be sure who we really are.

If any of this sounds familiar, remember this: you are not alone. Not even a little bit.

Many people experience these thoughts and feelings, and often the actions we take in life are based on this understanding.

When I started looking for answers, I expected to find them from older or more experienced people. But often it felt like even they did not have clear answers.

Sometimes it feels as if we might remain stuck in this confusion forever.

We begin by following what others do. We listen to what the elders suggest. We try to live up to what others expect of us.

But then a question appears inside us:

  • Is this really who I am?
  • Or am I simply living the way others see me or expect me to be?
  • And if that is true, then where am I in all of this?

Sometimes we wonder: Does it really matter if I become my real self? What exactly is the "real me"? How does it relate to the person I see every day? Is the voice inside my mind the real me?

Many times, we act on what our inner voice tells us. And that leads to one powerful question:

Who am I?

When I tried to find the answer, I began asking people around me who they thought I was.

But something interesting happened. Different people described me in different ways.

Some said I was foolish.
Some said I was honest.
Some said I was hardworking.
Some said I was a champion.

Everyone had a different opinion. And suddenly I felt confused.

Whom should I believe?
Whom should I follow?
Are they really correct?

Many of them did not even explore the question deeply. They did not fully understand my world, my thoughts, or my experiences. Yet their words began to shape how I saw myself.

For every word I heard, I started thinking that maybe I was that person.

Having so many different versions of myself felt painful. It was difficult to live one life while carrying so many different identities.

That is when I decided to explore the question for myself. Why do I feel this way?

Slowly, I realized something important. I was at a very special stage in my life. No longer a small child. Not quite an adult yet. It felt like standing at the doorway of my own life.

At this stage, we are often expected to behave in certain ways, even when we ourselves are still learning who we truly are.

Sometimes our minds become our biggest critics. We fill them with negative thoughts about ourselves.

But at the same time, there is another voice inside us: a quieter and wiser voice.

That voice sometimes tells us that we may be stronger than we believe. It whispers that we might have something powerful within us.

Some people call this intuition. Others call it inner wisdom.

I like to call it my Inner Light.

It is the quiet part of us that knows:

What feels right.
What feels wrong.
What makes your heart feel alive.
What makes your stomach twist with discomfort.

And here is the most exciting part. The more we learn to listen to this inner light, the stronger and braver we become.

In a way, it is like becoming a real-life superhero. Not the kind who flies through the sky or fights monsters. But the kind who understands emotions, sees truth clearly, chooses kindness, and learns how to face difficult situations without running away.

Being a real-life hero does not look like the heroes we see in movies, television shows, or videos. It begins with a simple choice: to take a step, or to entertain one's own thoughts.

Every person can grow. Growth does not mean becoming someone else. Growth means becoming more fully yourself.

The goal of this journey is not to turn you into a different person. The goal is to help you discover the strengths, values, and possibilities that already exist within you.

Do you know that superheroes know about themselves first? Every superhero you've ever heard of had a moment where they asked:

Who am I?
What makes me different?
Can I really change anything?

And they found their power not by copying others, but by looking within.

This book is going to help you do just that. We'll explore:

  • Thoughts and where they come from.
  • Feelings and how to understand them.
  • How to see yourself clearly.
  • How to understand others.
  • And how to build your own life tools.
But first, let's say this out loud together

"I'm not broken. I'm learning."
"I don't have to be like everyone else."
"I can be curious instead of confused."
"I can listen to myself, and trust what I hear."

So take a deep breath. This journey isn't about being perfect. It's about getting to know your real self: the one who's been with you all along.

Before turning the page, I want you to notice something.

For the last few minutes, while reading this chapter, your inner voice has been active. Maybe it agreed with some ideas. Maybe it questioned others. Maybe it reminded you of experiences from your own life.

That voice has been there the entire time.

The question is not whether it exists. The question is: Are you listening to it carefully?

And even more importantly: Who is listening to the voice?

That is where our journey begins.

Exploring Yourself: The Greatest Adventure You Will Ever Take

Before we continue our journey, let us pause for a moment.

Throughout life, we spend a great deal of time learning about the world around us. We learn mathematics, science, languages, sports, technology, and countless other subjects. We learn about famous people, countries, inventions, and discoveries. Yet one of the most important subjects is often missing from the timetable: ourselves.

Aarav was twelve years old and curious about everything. Every day after school, he watched videos, played games, and scrolled through social media. He admired athletes who won medals, creators who had millions of followers, and students who seemed to score perfect marks. Slowly, without realizing it, he began comparing himself with everyone he saw. Some days, he felt inspired. Other days, he felt small. The strange thing was that nothing bad had happened to him. Yet he often felt as if everyone else knew exactly who they were while he was still trying to figure himself out.

One evening, his grandfather noticed he was unusually quiet.

"What are you thinking about?" he asked.

"I don't know who I should become," Aarav replied. "Everyone seems better than me at something."

His grandfather smiled and asked a question that Aarav never forgot.

"You spend a lot of time looking at other people. How much time do you spend getting to know yourself?"

Aarav did not have an answer. That question is the beginning of this chapter.

Most people can describe their favorite actor, sportsperson, or friend. But when asked to describe themselves, they often hesitate. Why? Because understanding ourselves takes attention, patience, and honesty.

Before we begin exploring ourselves, let us prepare our minds.

Preparing To Meet Yourself

Before explorers begin a journey, they prepare their equipment. Before athletes compete, they warm up. Before musicians perform, they tune their instruments. Before we explore ourselves, we will prepare our attention.

Try it now, two minutes

Rub your hands together and notice the warmth. Look around and identify five things you can see. Listen carefully and notice sounds that you usually ignore. Notice any smells around you. Take a sip of water and pay attention to the taste.

Finally, notice how your body feels. Relaxed? Excited? Restless? Tired? There is no right answer. Notice.

Now take three slow breaths. Breathe in deeply. Hold for a moment. Breathe out slowly. Imagine your thoughts becoming a little quieter with each breath.

For the next two minutes, sit quietly. Do not try to stop your thoughts. Observe them. What is your mind talking about? What keeps appearing again and again? What emotions are present?

Congratulations. You have already started the journey of self-awareness.

Why We Look Outside For Answers

Human beings learn from others. Parents teach us how to walk and speak. Teachers help us understand the world. Friends influence our interests and habits. Books, videos, and technology expose us to new ideas. Learning from others is natural and helpful.

The challenge begins when we start believing that everyone else knows who we should be better than we do.

Many young people compare their appearance, achievements, talents, and popularity with those of others. Social media can make this even more difficult because people often share their highlights rather than their struggles. We see the victories but not the practice. We see the results but not the effort. We see the smiles but not always the challenges behind them.

Comparison can sometimes inspire growth. However, constant comparison can make us forget our own strengths.

Imagine a fish spending its entire life comparing itself to a bird. The fish may conclude that it has failed because it cannot fly. Yet the fish was never meant to fly. It was meant to swim.

In the same way, each person has unique strengths, interests, and possibilities.

Labels Are Not Identities

Have you ever noticed that different people describe you differently?

One person may call you quiet. Another may call you thoughtful. One teacher may see potential. Another may think you need more confidence. A friend may think you are funny. Someone else may think you are serious.

Which version is true? Perhaps all of them contain a small piece of the truth. Yet none of them completely defines who you are.

As we grow up, we collect labels. Some labels help us. Others limit us. If someone repeatedly tells a child that they are not good at something, the child may eventually believe it. If someone constantly praises a certain quality, the child may begin to identify entirely with it.

But labels are not identities.

You are more than your marks, talents, appearance, mistakes, successes, or failures. You are a growing person with the ability to learn, adapt, and change.

The Explorer's Journal

Take a notebook, or simply think about each question. There are no right answers.

Who am I?

  • What are three words that describe the real you?
  • What makes you different from everyone you know?
  • What do you enjoy doing even when nobody is watching?
  • What strengths do you already have that you sometimes forget about?

What do I feel?

  • What feeling visits you most often?
  • What emotions are difficult to express?
  • What helps you feel calm, safe, and understood?
  • What makes your heart feel full?

What do I love?

  • What activities make you lose track of time?
  • What topics make you curious?
  • What would you choose to do if you had an entire free day?

What do I fear?

  • What fear do you rarely talk about?
  • What helps you feel brave?
  • What would become possible if you faced that fear?

What do I believe?

  • What does being a good person mean to you?
  • What values matter most to you?
  • If you wrote a rulebook for your life, what would be the first rule?

What is my dream?

  • What kind of adult do you hope to become?
  • What do you want people to remember you for?
  • What positive difference would you like to make in the world?

The Day I Stopped Fighting Myself

For many years, people have struggled because they have tried to become someone else. They compare their journey to another person's. They compare their beginning with another person's middle.

One day, while walking through a garden, a child noticed roses, sunflowers, trees, and grass growing side by side.

The roses were not trying to become sunflowers. The trees were not trying to become grass. Each living thing was becoming the fullest version of itself.

That observation contained an important lesson.

Growth is not about becoming someone else. Growth is about becoming more fully yourself.

You are not every thought that enters your mind.
You are not every label people give you.
You are not every mistake you have made.
You are not every fear you carry.

You are much more than that.

You are your choices.
You are your values.
You are your courage.
You are your dreams.
You are your willingness to learn.

Mirror moment

Stand in front of a mirror. Take three slow breaths. Look at yourself with curiosity instead of judgment. Ask yourself:

  • What do I notice about myself today?
  • What is one thing I appreciate about who I am becoming?
  • What emotion is present right now?
  • If my heart could speak, what would it say?
  • What strength have I shown recently?
  • What challenge am I learning from?

Remember, the goal is not perfection. The goal is awareness.

What I Learned About Myself

Self-awareness is one of the greatest gifts we can develop. When we understand ourselves, we become less controlled by comparison, fear, confusion, and others' opinions.

We begin making choices that reflect who we truly are. We begin understanding our emotions instead of being controlled by them. We begin listening carefully to our inner voice while also questioning it wisely.

Most importantly, we begin treating ourselves with the same kindness and understanding that we often offer to others.

As you finish this chapter, remember these ideas:

Thoughts are not always facts.
Comparison is not the same as growth.
You do not need to copy others to be valuable.
Your journey is unique.
You are more capable than you realize.

And perhaps the most important lesson of all:

The greatest adventure is not exploring the world. The greatest adventure is exploring yourself.

Every day gives you a new opportunity to learn something about who you are. Every challenge teaches a lesson. Every success reveals a strength. Every mistake provides wisdom. Every question opens a door.

Keep exploring.
